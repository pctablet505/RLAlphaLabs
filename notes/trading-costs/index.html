What Trading Really Costs
on NSE Equities

Zero-brokerage marketing hides the four largest drags on Indian retail equity portfolios: statutory delivery taxes (22.55 bps), the flat ₹15.34 CDSL depository sell fee, order-book spreads (28–55 bps), and mandatory FIFO capital-gains tax realization.

Updated October 2026 · Public Statutory & Microstructure Reference

Most academic papers and retail backtests model transaction costs as a flat 10 to 15 basis points per trade and ignore capital-gains taxes altogether. On the National Stock Exchange of India (NSE), that assumption understates real friction by 3× to 8× for small and mid-sized accounts—turning strategies that look wildly profitable on paper into net money-losers in live demat accounts.

1. The Statutory Delivery (CNC) Floor: 22.55 bps Before Spreads

Even on a zero-brokerage discount platform, every cash-and-carry (CNC) equity delivery round-trip pays five mandatory statutory and regulatory levies governed by the Finance Act and exchange circulars:

  • Securities Transaction Tax (STT): 0.10% (10.00 bps) on the buy leg + 0.10% (10.00 bps) on the sell leg = 20.00 bps round-trip. Unlike intraday (MIS) trades where STT is only 0.025% on the sell side, delivery trades pay 8× higher STT.
  • Indian Stamp Duty: 0.015% (1.50 bps) charged on the buy leg.
  • NSE Exchange Transaction Charge: ~0.00322% (0.322 bps) per leg = 0.644 bps round-trip.
  • SEBI Turnover Fee & IPFT: ~0.0001% per leg = 0.02 bps round-trip.
  • 18% GST: Levied on brokerage, exchange turnover charges, SEBI fees, and depository charges (~0.12 bps proportional component).

Proportional FloorBefore a single paisa of bid-ask spread, market impact, or depository fee is counted, every NSE delivery round-trip starts at a hard statutory floor of 22.55 bps.

2. The Flat ₹15.34 CDSL DP Charge & The Retail Capital Scale Law

Whenever shares leave your demat account on a delivery sell, the depository participant (CDSL/NSDL + discount broker) debits a flat fee of ₹13.00 + 18% GST = ₹15.34 per scrip per day, regardless of whether you sold ₹2,000 or ₹20,00,000 worth of that stock.

Because ₹15.34 is a fixed rupee charge rather than a percentage, it creates a severe nonlinear penalty against small accounts and over-diversified baskets:

  • At ₹5,000 per stock position (e.g., a ₹1.5 Lakh account holding 30 stocks): ₹15.34 adds +30.68 bps of drag, pushing statutory round-trip costs from 22.25 bps to 52.93 bps before spreads.
  • At ₹10,000 per stock position (e.g., a ₹3 Lakh account holding 30 stocks): ₹15.34 adds +15.34 bps, bringing statutory round-trip cost to 37.89 bps.
  • At ₹50,000 per stock position (e.g., a ₹15 Lakh account holding 30 stocks): ₹15.34 shrinks to +3.07 bps, bringing statutory round-trip cost down to 25.62 bps.

Break-Even FloorAcross our capital-scale audits (including whole-share lot rounding and calibrated spreads), active multi-stock delivery portfolios face a structural break-even floor in the high lakhs. Below ₹3 Lakh, fixed per-scrip DP fees and integer-share rounding overwhelm even a +6%/yr gross signal.

3. Order-Book Spreads & Whole-Share Lot Rounding

Closing prices in daily OHLCV databases are last-traded prices, not executable quotes. Crossing the live NSE order book incurs an additional effective round-trip spread that scales with market-cap tier:

  • NIFTY 50 / Top-100 Liquid Large-Caps: 24–28 bps median round-trip effective spread + minimal market impact at retail ticket sizes.
  • Top-300 Mid-Cap Universe: 33.7 bps calibrated Corwin-Schultz / order-book round-trip spread.
  • Broad NIFTY 500 Tail (Ranks 301–500): 46.5–55.4 bps round-trip spread, with frequent 5% circuit-filter locks during earnings and macro shocks.

Additionally, Indian brokers do not support fractional shares. When an equal-weight ₹2 Lakh portfolio tries to allocate ₹6,667 into a stock trading at ₹4,200/share, integer-share rounding forces a 37% weight error on that name.

4. The Mandatory FIFO Tax Wedge: 20.8% STCG vs. 13.0% LTCG

Under Section 45(2A) of the Indian Income Tax Act and CBDT Circular 768, investors cannot cherry-pick which tax lots to sell (such as Highest-Cost-First). Every demat account is legally required to match sells against buys in strict First-In, First-Out (FIFO) order.

Under the Finance (No. 2) Act 2024 regime (including the 4% Health & Education Cess):

  • Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG, held ≤ 12 months, s.111A): Taxed at 20.0% × 1.04 = 20.8% at the end of every financial year.
  • Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG, held > 12 months, s.112A): Taxed at 12.5% × 1.04 = 13.0% above the ₹1.25 Lakh annual exemption.
  • The Passive Index Fund Advantage: A low-cost NIFTY 500 Index Fund rebalances internally without triggering investor-level capital gains tax, deferring taxation to a single terminal 13.0% LTCG redemption. Over 20.7 years (2006–2026), quarterly rebalancing of a direct 300-stock basket lost 2.14%/yr to cumulative transaction costs and annual FIFO STCG realizations—trailing the index fund in 69 of 70 (Capital × Breadth) tiers.

Portfolio RuleTo survive after tax, an active Indian equity system must keep annual turnover low, enforce hysteresis retention bands, and defer non-urgent sales of winning lots that are approaching the 365-day LTCG boundary.
